UNION REPRESENTATION

Weingarten Rights

Union-represented employees may have the right to request union representation during certain investigatory interviews that could lead to discipline.

Plain English

If management is questioning you as part of an investigation, and you reasonably believe the questioning could result in discipline, you may request that a union representative be present.

Important: The employee generally must request representation. Management is not required to automatically provide a union representative or remind the employee about this right.

When Might This Apply?

A Weingarten issue may arise when management asks questions to obtain information as part of an investigation and the employee reasonably believes the answers could result in discipline.

Not every conversation with management qualifies. The circumstances and purpose of the discussion matter.

What Can I Say?

“If this discussion could in any way lead to me being disciplined or terminated, I respectfully request that my union representative be present before I answer questions.”

What Should I Do?

Clearly request union representation. If you are unsure whether the discussion may involve discipline, contact a Local 8982 representative as soon as possible.

Official source: NLRB – Weingarten Rights

WORKING TOGETHER

Protected Concerted Activity

Federal labor law can protect employees who act together concerning wages, hours and other terms and conditions of employment.

Plain English

Employees may have protection when two or more employees act together concerning workplace issues, or when one employee acts on behalf of or seeks to involve other employees in a group workplace concern.

Examples may include: Talking with coworkers about workplace conditions, bringing a group concern to management, circulating a petition, or acting together concerning wages, hours, safety or other working conditions.

Important

Whether particular conduct is protected depends on the circumstances. If management questions or disciplines you concerning workplace activity with other employees, contact Local 8982 promptly.

Official source: NLRB – Employee Rights

PAY & WORKING CONDITIONS

Discussing Your Wages

Can Employees Talk About Pay?

Employees covered by the National Labor Relations Act generally have the right to communicate with coworkers about wages and other terms and conditions of employment.

Plain English: Simply because pay information is personal does not automatically mean employees lose federal labor-law protections when discussing their own wages with coworkers.

Situations Can Differ

Federal labor-law coverage and the circumstances of a particular workplace rule or conversation matter. Contact Local 8982 if you believe management is interfering with protected workplace discussions.

Official source: NLRB – Employee Rights

UNION REPRESENTATION

Duty of Fair Representation

The Union Has Responsibilities Too

A union that represents employees has a duty to represent employees in the bargaining unit fairly, in good faith and without unlawful discrimination.

This duty can apply to collective bargaining, grievance handling and other matters in which the union acts as the employees' exclusive bargaining representative.

Important: This does not necessarily mean every grievance must be filed, advanced or taken to arbitration. Decisions may depend on the facts, the collective bargaining agreement and the circumstances of the case.

Official source: NLRB – Right to Fair Representation

COMMONLY CONFUSED

Right-to-Work vs. At-Will

These terms deal with different legal concepts. Neither term, by itself, tells a Local 8982 member whether a particular discipline or discharge is permitted.

⚖️

Right-to-Work

Right-to-work generally concerns union membership or financial support as a condition of employment.

It does not mean: that an employee has a guaranteed “right to a job,” or that an employer automatically has an unlimited right to discipline or discharge an employee.
📋

At-Will Employment

At-will employment is a different employment-law concept involving when an employment relationship may be ended.

Important: Even where an at-will rule applies generally, applicable laws and enforceable contractual obligations may affect a particular termination.

LOCAL 8982 CONTRACT

Local 8982 Members Have Just-Cause Protection

Local 8982 members work under a collective bargaining agreement. The agreement requires just cause for discharge and provides contractual grievance procedures for disputes arising under the agreement.

Disciplined, Suspended or Discharged?

Do not assume that “Alabama is right-to-work” or general at-will employment principles end the conversation. Your union contract, the facts, applicable law and grievance deadlines may all matter. Contact Local 8982 promptly.

Why the Contract Matters

A collective bargaining agreement creates contractual rights and responsibilities between the employer and the union. Local 8982's agreement contains a discharge procedure requiring just cause, along with grievance and arbitration procedures for resolving covered disputes.

Plain English: “Right-to-work,” “at-will,” and “just cause” are not interchangeable terms. For a Local 8982 member facing discipline or discharge, the collective bargaining agreement can be critically important.

SAFETY & HEALTH CONCERNS

Reporting a Hazardous Condition

You Have the Right to Speak Up About Safety

MSHA states that miners have the right to file or make a complaint about a safety or health violation to a federal or state agency, the mine operator, an operator's agent or a miners' representative.

Anonymous Reporting to MSHA MSHA states that you do not need to identify yourself when reporting a hazardous condition through its hazardous-condition reporting system.

Local 8982 or MSHA?

You may report a safety concern to Local 8982 so the Safety Committee can review the condition. You may also report hazardous conditions directly to MSHA.

Submitting Local 8982's Safety Concern Report does not itself file an official complaint or formal inspection request with MSHA.

Report to Local 8982 Visit MSHA

Official source: MSHA – Miners' Rights and Responsibilities

REQUESTING AN MSHA INSPECTION

Section 103(g)

What Is a Formal 103(g) Request?

Section 103(g)(1) of the Mine Act provides that a miner or miners' representative may request an immediate MSHA inspection when there are reasonable grounds to believe that an imminent danger, a violation of the Mine Act, or a violation of a mandatory safety or health standard exists.

A Formal Request Has Requirements MSHA states that a formal Section 103(g)(1) request for inspection must be given to MSHA in writing and signed by the miner or miners' representative.

What About My Identity?

MSHA provides a copy of a formal 103(g)(1) complaint to the mine operator before or during the inspection. However, MSHA states that it takes reasonable steps to maintain the confidentiality of the complainant.

MSHA may remove the complainant's name and may rewrite information concerning a particular work area, equipment or work shift when those details could reveal the complainant's identity.

Important

A Local 8982 Safety Concern Report is not automatically a formal Section 103(g) request. Contact a Local safety representative or MSHA promptly if you believe a formal inspection request may be necessary.

Official source: MSHA – Hazardous Condition Complaints and Right to Request Inspections

RETALIATION & DISCRIMINATION

Section 105(c)

Protection for Exercising Mine Act Rights

MSHA states that Section 105(c) prohibits discrimination against miners, applicants for employment and representatives of miners for exercising statutory rights under the Mine Act.

Protected safety and health activity can include identifying hazards, requesting an MSHA inspection, making a safety or health complaint, participating in Mine Act proceedings, and qualifying refusals to engage in unsafe acts.

Examples Identified by MSHA Potentially discriminatory actions can include discharge, suspension, loss of pay, demotion, changes in pay or hours, reductions in benefits, vacation, bonuses or overtime, and interference with the exercise of Mine Act rights.
Time Can Matter

MSHA's miners' rights materials state that a discrimination complaint generally must be filed within 60 days of the discriminatory event. If you believe you are being retaliated against for protected mine-safety or health activity, contact Local 8982 and MSHA promptly.

Do not rely solely on Local 8982's online Safety Concern Report when you believe discrimination or retaliation may be occurring. An official Section 105(c) complaint is a separate MSHA process.

Official source: MSHA – Section 105(c) Discrimination Protections

UNSAFE OR UNHEALTHY CONDITIONS

Refusing Unsafe or Unhealthy Work

What Does MSHA Say?

MSHA states that miners have the right to refuse work in conditions they believe are unsafe, unhealthy or illegal after notifying the operator of the condition and giving the operator an opportunity to address the situation.

MSHA's Section 105(c) guidance also describes a protected work refusal as involving a good-faith, reasonable belief that a specific working condition threatens the miner's health or safety.

Specific Facts Matter

Do not reduce this right to “I can refuse any job I think is unsafe.” The condition, the miner's belief, notice to the operator, and other facts can matter. Notify the operator of the condition and contact a Local 8982 safety representative promptly.

Official sources: MSHA – Miners' Rights   |   MSHA – Section 105(c) Guidance

SAFETY REPRESENTATION

Miners' Representative Rights

What Is a Miners' Representative?

MSHA defines a miners' representative as a person, group or organization designated by two or more miners to represent their interests during mine safety and health enforcement processes.

What Can a Representative Do?

MSHA describes miners' representatives as having an important role in safety and health enforcement, including reporting hazards and unsafe conditions, providing worksite information to inspectors, and participating in inspection-related activities.

Why It Matters A miners' representative can bring practical knowledge of the workplace and miners' safety concerns into MSHA inspection and enforcement processes.

Official source: MSHA – Miners' Representative Guide
